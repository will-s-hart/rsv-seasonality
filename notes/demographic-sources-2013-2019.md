## Japan

- **2013 and 2014.** Use the original annual Population Estimates Prefectures Table 10 five-year age bands, with Table 4 as the same-release all-age check. Use the both-sex total-population series. The 2010–2015 intercensal revision adjusted prefecture population by sex during 2011–2014 and left age estimates as originally released. Matching the age band and denominator to the original vintage avoids introducing the later totals into those age shares. [2013 annual release](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/en/stat-search/files?layout=datalist&lid=000001118081), [2014 annual release](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/en/stat-search/files?layout=datalist&lid=000001132435), [2010–2015 revision methods](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/jinsui/2-5.html)
- **2015.** Use Census apportionment reference Table 2, published November 24, 2016. Use the total population across nationality and sex for both the five-year age band and all-age count. The bureau allocated unknown age and nationality proportionally within municipalities and summed those results to prefectures. It later published a retrospective Census table under a different unknown-data method. The 2016–2019 estimate revision used the 2015 Census with unknown age prorated as its starting point. [2015 Census apportionment methods](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/kokusei/2015/kekka/anbun.html), [reference tables](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/files?layout=datalist&tclass1=000001090735&tclass2=000001090736&tstat=000001080615), [2015–2020 revision methods](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/jinsui/2-6.html)
- **2016–2019.** Use the October 1 prefecture estimates in Table 10 and the same-release totals in Table 5 from the February 3, 2022 intercensal release. The age-specific revision apportions the discrepancy between the 2020 estimates and Census. Record the difference in method between original estimates, the 2015 Census reference and revised estimates, including each table's units and precision. [2015–2020 revision catalogue](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/en/stat-search/files?layout=datalist&lid=000001287677&page=1)

Published **all-age controls for later integration** are 127,298 thousand persons nationally in the original 2013 release and 127,083 thousand in the original 2014 release. The 2015 Census reports 127,094,745 persons in Japan and 13,515,271 in Tokyo. The revised national October 1 totals for 2016–2019 are 127,042, 126,919, 126,749, and 126,555 thousand persons, respectively. Compare these with the source's national lines at their published precision. Test the agreement of prefecture all-ages between the age and totals tables separately and account for independently rounded displays before comparing sums to national totals. The summary controls do not establish the age-0–4 values or the units of individual worksheet cells. [2013 summary](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/jinsui/2013np/index.html), [2014 summary](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/jinsui/2014np/index.html), [2015 Census summary](https://www.stat.go.jp/english/data/kokusei/2015/final_en/pdf/s01.pdf), [2016–2019 revised summary](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/jinsui/pdf/2-6.pdf)

## United States and Puerto Rico

The Population Estimates Program released the 2010–2020 intercensal demographic series on September 30, 2025. Use July 1, 2013–2019. The state file layout specifies geography as of 2020. The command selects documented state-level rows with total sex (`SEX=0`) and total Hispanic origin (`ORIGIN=0`). It adds the six mutually exclusive race groups across ages 0–4 for the numerator and across all ages (0 through the 85+ group) for the denominator. Require each state/DC, race and age cell exactly once. The national age-by-sex CSV supplies the same-release all-age and ages 0–4 controls for each year. Its all-age row must also agree with its own complete age distribution. The official methodology describes controlling within the geographic and demographic hierarchy. Confirm exact agreement with the real CSVs before locking the files. [Release](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/intercensal-estimates-demographics.html), [methodology](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/technical-documentation/methodology/intercensal/2010-2020-intercensal-estimates-methodology.pdf), [state layout](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/technical-documentation/file-layouts/2010-2020/intercensal/state/SC-EST2020INT-ALLDATA6.pdf), [national control layout](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/technical-documentation/file-layouts/2010-2020/intercensal/national/NC-EST2020INT-AGESEX-RES.pdf), [Census state FIPS codes](https://www.census.gov/library/reference/code-lists/ansi/ansi-codes-for-states.html)

National sums can agree when counts move between states. With `--check-counties`, the build checks each state/DC against the sums of `POPESTIMATE` and `AGE04_TOT` in the separately published United States county age-by-sex file. It uses Census `YEAR` codes 5–11 for July 1, 2013–2019 and checks county-year uniqueness and coverage. This comparison uses a separate Census table from the same release and geography vintage. Both tables are from the same statistical program. The state and control source entries and staged receipts are tied to an explicit Census release ID. [County table catalogue](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/popest/intercensal-2010-2020-counties-detail.html), [county file layout](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/technical-documentation/file-layouts/2010-2020/intercensal/county/CC-EST2020INT-AGESEX.pdf)

## Reproduce locally

Use Python 3.11 or later. [The source contract](../data/demographic-sources.toml) records source IDs, official download endpoints, coverage, vintages, release dates, units, and attribution links.

```sh
python3 scripts/demographic_sources.py stage us-states
python3 scripts/demographic_sources.py stage us-national-control
python3 scripts/demographic_sources.py stage us-county-control
python3 scripts/demographic_sources.py build-us --check-counties
python3 scripts/demographic_sources.py stage pr-commonwealth
python3 scripts/demographic_sources.py stage jp-2013-age
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 -m unittest discover -s scripts -p 'test_demographic_sources.py'
```

Run `stage --help` for the remaining source IDs. The `stage` command also accepts `--from-file PATH` for unchanged local bytes and marks the receipt `local_file`. It keeps original bytes and their JSON receipt together under `data/raw/demography/<source-id>/`. The receipt includes the byte count, SHA-256, capture time and method, requested URL, resolved download URL if present, available HTTP metadata, table/vintage, geography, source units, and reference dates. An observed checksum identifies the staged bytes. The optional `expected_sha256` field in the current contract determines whether the stage command checks and reports a pin. The receipt retains the pin setting at capture time. Rerunning `stage` for a cached source verifies its checksum and reports its original capture method. Use a separate cache to fetch fresh bytes or to re-stage a Census receipt that predates explicit release IDs. XLSX staging checks for a workbook and a linked worksheet. Japan spreadsheet entries also accept the legacy Excel file signature. [SpreadsheetML workbook structure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/office/open-xml/spreadsheet/structure-of-a-spreadsheetml-document)

The build rechecks staged digests, checks the published layouts, and validates age and geography coverage and the national controls before atomically writing `data/processed/demography/us-states.csv`. The CSV contains year, date, geography (`US-xx`, two-digit FIPS), name, counts in persons, a fraction for ages 0–4, universe, vintage, release date and ID, and input digests and capture methods. A build without `--check-counties` leaves the county provenance fields blank and establishes only national aggregate agreement. Raw and processed data live in Git-ignored directories.

The synthetic fixtures exercise the builders and their rejection checks. On department, the three official Census CSVs were staged with local SHA-256 receipts. The state and national files decoded as UTF-8, while the county file contained a legacy-encoded byte in Doña Ana County and is read as Windows-1252. The staged inputs passed `build-us --check-counties`, producing 357 state/DC–year rows and matching both the national and county controls for July 1, 2013–2019.

## Natality follow-up

Audit natality as a separate source, retaining the birth event period and mother's geography of residence where available. For 2013–2019 CDC WONDER, Region 2 natality consists of New York and New Jersey. HHS Region 2 also includes Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Audit the territorial releases separately before treating any birth aggregate as full HHS Region 2. For Japan, verify the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare Vital Statistics table's nationality coverage, whether prefecture refers to residence or occurrence, and whether counts include births abroad or foreign-national births in Japan. Document the selected release and its match to the population universe before deriving natality measures.
